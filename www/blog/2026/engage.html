Burger.

Unfortunately, nobody will be left behind.

Written February 19, 2025

If men learn this, it will implant forgetfulness in their souls. They will cease to exercise memory because they rely on that which is written, calling things to remembrance no longer from within themselves, but by means of external marks.

What you have discovered is a recipe not for memory, but for reminder. And it is no true wisdom that you offer your disciples, but only the semblance of wisdom, for by telling them of many things without teaching them you will make them seem to know much while for the most part they know nothing. And as men filled not with wisdom but with the conceit of wisdom they will be a burden to their fellows.

This was one of Plato's argument against writing. That writing information down wouldn't help people remember, just remind them. To him, it was a vital difference. If you don't internalize what you learn, instead relying on something external to keep track of it, then you haven't truly learned anything.

The irony is that I can only recall his words from written work. It's hard to argue writing has been bad for philosophy, science, and humanity as a whole. The sheer breadth of ideas we can examine, sometimes thousands of miles and years away from their sources, is incredible. And having those ideas written as things we can manipulate was vital for more advanced science and math. And yet, he wasn't wrong. Oral traditions involved memorizing long stories and songs to preserve them for generations. Can you imagine memorizing the entirety of Homer's epics and reciting them, word for word, to pass it on to the next generation?

I recently watched a YouTube video that I found moving. A week or so later, I wanted to watch it again. However, I forgot the title of the video. I also forgot the name of its creator. No matter, I probably added it to my Favorites. I didn't. Well, surely I liked the video. I checked my list of liked videos. There were hundreds from the past week. I didn't remember watching most of them. Do I really watch so many shorts? I couldn't find the one I was looking for. Was it removed? I give up the search.

Another week or so later, I was still bothered by this. If I can't find a video I enjoyed just two weeks ago, that meaningfully spoke to me, what does that say about me? I redoubled my efforts. I subscribed to the animator, maybe I'll recognize him in my subscriptions. When did I subscribe to so many hundreds of channels? I don't even watch most of these people. He also made a video where a student gives a presentation on chicken farming. I also forgot that video's title. I tried searching by its content: classroom, battery cages, surreal animation. I ask ChatGPT, giving it a summary. It confidently gives me the title of a film that doesn't exist.

Eventually, I find the video about chickens in my Favorites playlist: “Existential Crisis in Class” by WhatsItLike. I find the video I was looking for: “Tears in the Robot Factory”.

I didn't remember the names, just recalled them.

Nobody told me I had to get a smartphone or be “left behind”. It was a more useful tool that I wanted. Large language models are a very impressive technology, useful for specific applications. They aren't useful for everything, and they aren't human-level.

I believe AI has so many pushy, nasty advocates because it's a matter of ego. They don't want to be seen as stupid for relying on it. In fact, it's everybody else who's stupid for staying behind. So I'll say what I think, and what they seem to fear: if you use AI and like its output, my first assumption is that you're an idiot.

I imagine you writing two sentences of thought to generate paragraph after paragraph of mealy-mouthed nonsense, and then saying to yourself “you took the words right out of my mouth!”

I imagine you generating thousands of lines of code you don't understand, then burdening the people around you to figure out if it works.

I imagine you flooding art websites with thirty variations of trashy art you prompted, incapable of even curating its output.

I imagine you running to ChatGPT at the first sign of difficulty in any task, becoming incapable of learning, and spending the rest of your life paying a subscription for the ability to think.

Large language models have gotten larger, and their output has gotten better, and it will probably continue getting better. At some point, they may actually be able to do things I've tried to use them fore, like getting a list of books about a particular topic (they give me irrelevant or fake books), help me with coding problems (if I can't figure it out from a web search, the AI generates fake results), finding old media from vague descriptions (again, a web search is just as helpful).
